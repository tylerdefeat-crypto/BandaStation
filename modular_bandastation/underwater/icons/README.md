# Underwater artwork

`seafloor.png` and `sources/water_volume.png` were produced with the built-in ImageGen tool. The seafloor was revised against the user's first underwater canyon reference, with darker values and distant ruins. The water source is a 4×4 sheet of 16 consecutive dark teal water-volume frames, using the seafloor as its palette reference. `water.dmi` contains the 32×32 animation (0.2s per frame, unlimited loop with rewind) and an opaque `ocean_mask` state for the native parallax whitifier. That mask is below parallax; transparency is applied only to the animated overlay above it. `export.py` crops, resizes and packages the generated frames using the repository's `tools.dmi`; it does not repaint them. Its assertions check the animation, opacity, palette and small differences between adjacent frames.

`haze.png`, `fine_silt.png` and `silt.png` are unchanged transparent layers from the project's existing `_maps/map_files/syndie_underwater/underwater_layers` asset pack. Their in-game opacity, relative speed and drifting animation are set in `../code/parallax.dm`. All runtime paths point inside this module.

The seabed is intentionally **not tiled**. The renderer sizes one image to cover the view and camera travel. The atmospheric layers use native 480px tiling. The water tile has no baked caustics or light beams; the existing caustics and projector modules provide those effects.

## Final seafloor edit prompt

Use case: style-transfer. Image 1 is the EXISTING BACKGROUND TEXTURE TO REWORK. Image 2 is the user's STRONG COMPOSITION AND MOOD REFERENCE. Repaint image 1 substantially so it is much closer to image 2, while darker, gloomier, and still suitable as a top-down underwater game parallax. Output ONE square opaque seamless repeating texture, not a screenshot. Adopt the reference's LARGE cliff shoulders, shadowed chasms and broad silt-filled channels, distant fractured stone ledges and a few buried rectilinear slab / wreck silhouettes, all seen vertically down through dense water. Avoid the small evenly scattered rubble and uniform gravel-like composition of image 1. Create a strong spatial sense of looking down a deep canyon: dark massive rock shapes frame irregular open cloudy channels, with murky layered suspended sediment obscuring the bottom. Shapes and ruins must be diffuse and far away, seen through a thick column of water, never crisp foreground platforms. Use the reference's dark petrol teal/cyan family rather than cobalt blue, but significantly reduce light and contrast versus image 2: nearly black blue-teal cliff shadows, dark desaturated cyan haze, very restrained diffuse illumination. Foreboding quiet ocean depth, no monsters/horror motifs. Mostly shadow and haze; bottom features are barely visible, but unmistakably underwater rather than space. Preserve a readable broad composition at final 480x480 cell size. Critical: opposite image edges must connect seamlessly in both axes, NO vignette/border, no seams when repeated. No submarine, vehicle, beam of light, bubbles, creatures, surface waves, white foam, ripples, stars, sky, horizon, text or watermark. Dynamic lights, caustics, particles will be separate engine layers, do not bake those into this base. Full bleed game background only.

The final renderer uses the full source as a single image, rather than relying on the generation prompt's seamless-repeat request.

## Raised rock crests

`seafloor_crests.png` is a transparent 1254×1254 ImageGen edit of `seafloor.png`. It preserves the three large foreground formations and highlights their upper shoulders, leaving the channels transparent. The original seabed is unchanged. Both images use the same scaling, centering and speed so the highlights stay registered instead of producing double cliffs. The bottom retains `#303E46`; the raised stone uses `#A8CDD6`. Both pass through ordinary lighting, including flashlight falloff; neither is fullbright. High quality now draws five layers (bottom, crests, haze, fine silt, silt). Lower settings retain both terrain layers and reduce the atmosphere layers; disabling parallax still hides the entire background.

## Water-volume animation edit prompt

Use case: style-transfer. Asset type: aligned animation sprite sheet for a 32x32 top-down game WATER VOLUME overlay. Image 1 is the old blue WATER texture edit target. Image 2 is the existing SEAFLOOR PALETTE REFERENCE, do not include seabed objects in the water. Replace image 1 with ONE square sprite sheet, EXACTLY 4 columns by 4 rows of equal square cells, 16 animation frames in reading order, touching without gaps or borders. Every cell shows the SAME quiet, extremely low contrast cloudy water-volume material at consecutive very small time increments. Animation: slowly flowing diffuse soft wisps of suspended sediment, tiny gradual shifts and soft morphing, not random different textures. Frame 1 through 16 describe a slow smooth oscillation, no sudden changes, enough similarity that the animation looks calm when reduced to 32x32 per cell. Each frame must tile seamlessly spatially on both opposite edges, uniform edge brightness, no vignette. Color family must match image 2: very dark desaturated petroleum teal/cyan, near-black blue teal with muted blue-green wisps; NO cobalt/royal blue, no bright green. Texture is a horizontal SLICE THROUGH the interior of a deep water column, never the water surface. Broad hazy diffuse translucent-looking cloudy forms, very restrained contrast, essentially dark teal fog material. Opaque full bleed sprite sheet; transparency is applied by the game engine. No drawn frame borders, guides, labels or numbers. No foam, ripples, caustics, waves, bubbles, stars, hard particles, light beams, shimmer, surface highlights, rocks, seafloor, animals, objects, text or watermarks. Do not render the game's scene or a preview mockup: return only the 4x4 production animation spritesheet.
